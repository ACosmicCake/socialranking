Frequently asked questions

Can I trust this quiz?

Treat it as a well-sourced estimate, not a measurement. Every comparison uses published data from the World Bank, the UN system, Gallup or UBS, and the methodology sets out each step and its limits. The weakest links are self-reported answers, data years that range from 2015 (education) to 2025, and the fact that household surveys under-count the very rich.

How is socioeconomic status (SES) usually measured?

Researchers typically combine education, income and occupation. Some also use household size, access to resources or subjective social status {{ cite("apa_ses") }}. Occupation has no comparable worldwide data, so this quiz uses income, education, living standards, financial security and (optionally) wealth.

Why does my country matter?

The same amount of money buys very different things in different countries. Your income is converted into "international dollars" with a purchasing-power-parity factor, so $10 means what $10 bought in the United States in 2017. Your country also determines which national distribution you're compared with for the "within your country" result.

Why per person, and why per day?

That's how the World Bank measures poverty and inequality: household income (or spending) divided by the number of people in the household, expressed per day {{ cite("pip") }}. A household of four on $3,000 a month lives on about $25 per person per day.

Why is my "world" result so much higher than my result within my country?

For most people in middle- and high-income countries, global inequality between countries is much larger than inequality within them. Someone at the median in a high-income country is typically among the richest fifth of the world.

My country isn't accepting an exact income. Why?

Converting today's income into 2017 prices needs a consumer price series and a PPP factor. Where these are missing, or inflation has been above 100% a year, the conversion isn't reliable, so we ask for a range instead.

Do you store my answers?

Only if you tick the box at the end. Even then we store coarse, derived values only (for example your income rounded to a power-of-two band in international dollars, never the amount you typed), with no name, IP address or exact time. Aggregates appear on the responses page for groups of at least 5 people.

Is there an API?

Yes. POST /api/score with a JSON object using the same field names as the form (see the questionnaire), for example {"country": "VNM", "income_mode": "band", "income_band": "b4", "education": "secondary"}.
